## 2.1 Inspection of all idler rollers

*Rollers should always be inspected on operational AVI routes. We should trust that a well executed AVI inspection during the operational period will highlight any issues with rollers.*

All rollers should be free turning and free from wear. Seized idler rollers is one of the largest causes of belt wear. Disk style return rollers have been used in the past as they have less surface area in contact with the belt, therefore having less impact on tracking of the belt. A disadvantage of using disk style return rollers is that if the tracking does decentralise then the end disks could trap the belt therefore not allowing it to track back to centre. More recently parallel style rollers have been used as return rollers. A parallel style of roller has the advantage that it will not trap the belt but it will have more impact on tracking as it is in contact with the full width of the belt. A parallel roller also has the effect of dewatering the belt therefore it is important that the belt scrapers are in good condition.

*It would be the preferred option that the cell owner for the belt in question does the final AVI route to be comfortable with the additional scope of work for the maintenance period.*

*If any rollers cannot be inspected during the operational period then these will need to be unguarded and manually inspected by turning and looking/listening/feeling for defects once the belt has been isolated.*

Consideration should be given at all times to remaking guarding etc. to allow for all rollers to be able to be visually inspected whilst belt is running. This will allow more thorough AVI routes to be completed and will reduce PM time in the maintenance period due to having to remove less guarding etc. to complete inspections.

*Where possible we should look at the possibility of fitting Smiley Monroe rollers (and transom units if appropriate). All sites should create a list of rollers required for each belt. This document should be circulated to all people that it would benefit and spare rollers etc. should be available and easily locatable in the stores.*

*More in depth roller selection tools can be found in Martins Foundation chapter 10. This can be found in appendix 1. This wouldn’t be necessary for normal maintenance practises but if problems are encountered then some design work should be considered.*

*All the mentioned drums should be inspected on AVI routes. A more in depth inspection may be required at the beginning of the maintenance period as drums might not be easily accessible during operations to do these inspections.*

- *lagging should be inspected to ensure even wear across the pulleys. Lagging should also be inspected to ensure it is well fixed to the drum.*
- *Drums should be checked that they are in full contact with the belt and the belt is centred on the drum. (assuming proper alignment of drums)*
- *Check keyways etc on drum for signs of wear which could lead to the drum moving on the shaft.*

### Inspection of split bearings will comprise of the following but method to be agreed with site engineering team:

- **CBM (ultrasound/vibration analysis) inspection of bearings during the campaign to gain knowledge of condition of bearing and baseline.**
- **Any bearings that have been flagged as having an issue by CBM techniques to be split for further inspection and regreasing.**
- **Inspection of seals and signs of water ingress etc. when the inspection is completed.**
- **Based upon risk some sites may opt to remove the top from cooper bearings to inspect.**

---

### Installation of new bearings

Installation of new bearings should be done in the most controlled environment possible. This is not always possible when working on a conveyor due to the nature of the equipment. It is therefore advised that we do not split bearings to inspect as when reinstalling we give rise to maintenance induced faults. (split bearings would only be split to be inspected if there is any evidence that there could be an issue with the bearing.)

---

*If there is a concern that a bearing could be affected by water ingress etc. then an analysis should be done on why there would be possible water ingress (eliminate the source) or bearing selection should be considered.*

*Cantley has done some trialling of new bearing technologies and attached in appendix 4 is some information of Timken bearings and housings that have been used. The advantage of these type of bearings is that the seal is on the inner race of the bearing therefore a damaged shaft will not affect the sealing integrity of the bearing. These bearings would be particularly suited to an application where there is a lot of water present or if there is a vapour coming off the product that would ingress into a more poorly sealed bearing.*

---

it is also important to have a robust lubrication schedule which will give bearings the longest life span possible and minimise the risk of failures during the operational period.

CBM is the preferred technique for monitoring the condition of the bearings. There are various differing techniques that can be used including vibration analysis and ultrasound to monitor condition.

It is important to take baseline readings of all bearings that are to be inspected. The CBM equipment that is used must have a function to take quantitative data and this result should be recorded as part of the inspection. ### 2.5 Inspect belt condition and condition of vulcanised joint

It is important to inspect the belt joint at least annually. The belt must be stopped to do a thorough visual examination of the joint.

Inspecting the vulcanised joint on conveyor belts is a service that our belting suppliers often offer.

*In order for the belt joint to be inspected the belt should be parked in a convenient position so that a person can visually inspect both sides of the vulcanised joint. Use line marker or something similar to mark the joint to make it easy to find for whoever is going to do the inspection.*

*The Vulcanised joint should be inspected for wear and separation. If there are any signs of this then a decision should be made as to whether the joint should be remade or whether it is worth putting a new belt on the conveyor.*

*It is also important to check the spliced joint throughout the operational period for signs of degradation of the joint. If there is a concern over the condition of the joint during the operational period then it may be worth getting an expert opinion on a repair method suitable to see the belt through to the next available maintenance period.*

### 2.6 Inspect scraper for wear and position/alignment

It is important to keep the scraper in good condition to ensure efficient cleaning of the belt. During the operational period we can do a visual inspection of the belt to assess the efficiency of the scraper. There should be little carryover onto the floor if the scraper is performing well. Any scraper issues should be picked up on AVI routes as there will be evidence of material carryover on the underside of the belt and on the floor.

**When doing the annual inspection:**

- *Ensure angle of scraper is correct (consult manufacturer to get correct angle)*
- *Ensure tension of scraper on belt is correct. It is important to ensure this is correct so that blade wear is kept to a minimum whilst belt cleaning is at its most efficient. More modern scrapers will have a tensioning unit to keep the correct amount of tension on the belt*
- *Ensure that scraper is parallel to belt (this should be correct from installation)*
- *Ensure blades are not overly worn and ensure that blades are worn evenly (check with manufacturer for wear rates)*

## 2.7 Inspection of clean flows

Due to the running nature of cleanflow conveyors, they are difficult to inspect during the operational period. It is therefore important to thoroughly inspect the cleanflow during the maintenance period to ensure trouble free operation. A poorly maintained cleanflow can also be a major contributor to premature belt wear.

A cleanflow system can also be a major contributor to water usage on site so this usage should be monitored and kept as low as possible without having a negative impact on belt wear. Consideration should be given to installing a timer on the spray bar operation to allow an amount of water on the belt without being excessive.

The below points should be considered when applying a maintenance plan to cleanflow systems:

- **Once all guards are removed do a thorough visual inspection of the cleanflows checking for holes or high wear points.**
- **Use ultrasonic thickness testing measurements to map overall wear of the cleanflow at regular intervals (this might be yearly or longer depending on wear rates). Once the thickness of the cleanflows and the wear rates are known we can make a decision on when the cleanflows need to be changed based on evidence.**
- **Check for any damage/wear to the belt caused by the cleanflows**
- **Inspect cleanflow water spray bar for correct operation and signs of leakage.**

## 2.8 Inspect tensioning unit for correct operation/inspect belt tension

Correct belt tension is important to give the belt pre tension ensuring that

- The driving pulley will drive the belt under all running conditions
- That the sag between carrying and return rollers is limited causing minimum resistance.

The correct tension for a belt can be calculated based on numerous factors but on an inspection level we should ensure that there is no evidence that the drive pulley has been slipping on the belt and there isn’t excessive sag between return rollers.

*Depending on type of operation, belt tensioning is split into two groups. Fixed belt tensioning and self-adjusting belt tensioning.*

## 2.9 Inspect tracking rollers/tracking

A conveyor belt will always move to the side with more friction on the belt.

### Diagram: Conveyor Belt Tracking

- **Type:** Schematic/illustration
- **Components:**
  - Conveyor belt
  - Three orange rollers (idler rollers)
  - Green arrow indicating direction of belt movement
  - Text annotation: "The fundamental rule of conveyor belt tracking is that the belt moves toward the high-friction side of the belt."
- **Purpose:** Demonstrates that a conveyor belt will always move to the side with more friction.

---

There are many things that can cause the belt to track so setup is important to ensure the belt runs straight.

- All idler rollers and load rollers should be correctly aligned so that the belt does not want to wander. This should be ensured when replacing transom sets or idler rollers and when replacing head drums or tail drums alignment checks should be performed.
- Faults with the structure or transom units etc. could allow the belt to wander to one side.
- The belt could be damaged or have a faulty spliced joint allowing the belt to wander.
- The material being loaded onto the belt could have an affect on how the belt tracks. If it is not loaded centrally then the belt will track over to one side.

Most of the above points are more down to the design of the conveyor belt and should be considered if persistent problems are being had with belt tracking. More information can be found in martins foundation chapter 16.

## 2.10 Inspect side skirts and wear liners

Wear liners provide a sacrificial surface that minimises spillage at belt transfer points. They will usually be mounted for the first section of the belt once the load has been transferred onto the belt and it aides in centralising the load onto the belt. Side skirts will often be fixed to the wear liner to prevent spillage off the belt.

**When inspecting the wear liners and side skirts:**

- *Visually inspect the belt side of the wear liners for any spots of high wear. If liners look thin do a thickness check to help consideration of replacing liners.*
- *Visually inspect side skirts and back sealing skirts. If there is a visible gap between the skirts and the belt adjustment should be considered. If a side skirt is worn past the adjustable level it will require replacement.*

Side skirts should not be replaced with old bits of belt as this could cause damage to the conveyor belt. A material with less abrasion resistance than the specific conveyor belt material should be used.

*New wear liners can be purchased from smiley Monroe or Apex belting or other belting suppliers.*

The majority of these inspections can be implemented in the operational period on the AVI inspections. Jobs should be raised for any issues that need to be addressed in the maintenance period.

## 2.11 Inspect for signs of leakage from sides of conveyor

This inspection is best completed during the operational period during the AVI route.

### 2.12 Inspect transfer/discharge chutes

Transfer chutes will normally be made from a metal construction. The aim for the transfer chute is to move material from the end of one conveyor into the next part of the process. This could be onto another conveyor belt or onto a different piece of plant.

*When loading from one conveyor belt to another a transfer point is best designed if:*
- It loads the material onto the centre of the belt in at a uniform rate.
- Its orientated in the direction of belt travel and allows loading at the same speed of the belt being loaded.
- It should load the belt once the belt is fully troughed and with minimum impact force on the belt.

The transfer point is a very important part of the conveying system as this is where the belt receives most of its abrasion and practically all impacting.

During the operational period visual checks should be made on the transfer points if they are visible.

- *No signs of blockage in the discharge chute which could begin to bridge the product.*
- *No visual signs of wear on the discharge chute which will mean leakage of the product onto the floor.*
- *Ensure product is loading centrally onto belt. If product is not loading centrally and causing tracking issues then this would be a design task to change the loading profile.*

In the maintenance period the inspection type should change as we should now have full visibility of the transfer chute.

There will be points on the chute that will be the high wear points as they will have had the impacting of the product. Once these points have been identified we can use different maintenance strategies to identify possible issues and rectify.

- *Visually inspect for signs of wear and if points of high wear are visible then do ultrasonic thickness mapping in this area to ensure chute integrity for the coming operational period.*

## 2.13 Inspect impact units

Transfer points will ordinarily be designed to minimise the amount of impact onto the belt being loaded. This is not always possible and in these instances impact units will be used to absorb the impact forces of the load falling onto the belt. On British Sugar sites impact units are mainly found on beet belts due to the distance that the beet must fall out of the dewatering drum onto the conveyor belt.

### Image: Impact Cradle Diagram (Figure 10.30)
- **Description**: The image shows an impact cradle composed of a steel framework holding a set of impact-absorbing bars. The bars combine a slick top surface and one or more sponge-like secondary layers to absorb the energy of impact.
- **Text in image**: 
  - "Impact cradles are composed of a steel framework holding a set of impact-absorbing bars. The bars combine a slick top surface and one or more sponge-like secondary layers to absorb the energy of impact."
- **Purpose**: To illustrate the construction and function of an impact cradle used under conveyor belts.

They will usually comprise a slick top layer capable of taking the impact (UHMW commonly used) and allowing the belt to slide over with as little friction as possible. This will be mounted on a softer layer which will dampen the impact of the load onto the belt.

### Image: Impact Cradle Installation Diagram (Figure 10.36)
- **Description**: The image shows a cross-sectional view of an impact cradle installed under a conveyor belt.
- **Text in image**: 
  - "Impact cradles are usually installed so that the bars in the center of the cradle are set 12 to 25 millimeters (0.5 to 1 in.) below the normal unloaded line of the belt."
- **Purpose**: To show the correct installation height of the impact cradle relative to the belt.

The impact unit will usually be mounted just below the level of the belt so that the belt can take some of the impact of the load and the impact unit will dampen the rest of the impact.

It is difficult to inspect an impact module during the operational period due to the position underneath the belt.

*In the maintenance period a visual inspection should take place of the impact unit. It is important to follow manufacturers guidelines for allowable wear on the bars etc. and these should be replaced once they get to the maximum permissible wear.*

*Alignment of the impact unit should also be checked to ensure it sits just off the belt when the belt is not loaded to minimise the amount of friction between the belt and the unit.*

### 2.15 Inspection of Gearboxes

All conveyor gearboxes should be on AVI routes throughout the campaign period and should also be on a lubrication route and vibration analysis route.

All gearboxes should be laser aligned to head drums and all motors that are not coupled on a spigot should be laser aligned to the gearbox. Any bedplate modifications that are necessary to make this possible should be raised as maintenance tasks and completed.

During the maintenance period there is limited inspections that are necessary on gearboxes but the following could be considered as good practise:

- *Remove guards and visually inspect couplings between gearbox and head drum and motor and gearbox. Ensure no excessive backlash in any couplings and raise job for retrospective work that needs to be completed. If a motor is directly coupled to a gearbox using a spider coupling etc. then this should not be removed and we can rely on vibration analysis to give us early signs of coupling faults.*
- *Visually inspect gearbox for signs of oil leaks or other damage and raise work orders as required.*
