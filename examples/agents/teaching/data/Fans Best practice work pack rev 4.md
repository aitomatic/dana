### 1.0 Justification for Best Practice Document

This document is not meant to replace STD-ENG-015, but supplement it with information gathered through experience within the Mechanical Engineering group.

The document is written in a way that further sections can be added with time as further experience, knowledge and understanding is gained in the field of Centrifugal Fans.

The document is aimed to be a guide to give clarity and understanding where previous mistakes may have been made. The guide is not in place to enable an engineer to design a fan, but raise the right questions to the OEM, when specifying a new fan for manufacture and ensure the correct level of maintenance is carried out on installed equipment.

---

### 2.0 Health and Safety Considerations

Inspection and maintenance of fans can pose significant risks to employees as these activities bring the workers into close proximity of the fans.

All maintenance tasks should be done under a task based risk assessment and isolated to relevant British Sugar standards by competent personnel. For further risk assessment information please see **PR-HSM-200** in the DMS. If anybody is unsure of the isolations on the equipment then work **should not** commence until everybody is comfortable. For further guidance on safe isolation of equipment please see **GD-ENG-008** in the DMS.

### Balancing of Fans

When outsourcing the balancing of a fan, **G2.5 (<0.8mm/s RMS)** should be specified as the acceptance criteria. However, in line with the minimum standard set out in STD-ENG-015, this can be relaxed to **G6.3 (<2.2mm/s RMS)** when undertaking onsite, in situ, balancing.

### 4.2 Motion Amplification – How and Why it should be used

#### Motion Amplification Technology

Motion amplification technology gives the ability to visualise vibration motion and quickly identify where the source of vibration is coming from and is especially useful for high 1x vibration frequencies, looseness, and soft foot type faults where standard vibration analysis cannot pinpoint issues.

British Sugar has invested in motion amplification technology and holds the necessary equipment to survey a large range of operating plant. The equipment is held corporately at the Wissington site with the responsible owner being the CBM engineer role.

**It is highly recommended that when fans are first installed, a base line reading/survey is conducted for future reference and troubleshooting.**

### 4.3 Rules on Online Cleaning

During normal operations it is possible for products to slowly coat the surface of rotating parts of centrifugal fans, just as much as the stationary elements, and give rise to vibration when either a small section falls off or the build up is uneven. To reduce the impact of vibration it may become necessary to clean the built up coating off, and whilst this is ideally done with the fan off-line, this may not be practical due to the impact on production. Cleaning whilst the fan is still on-line and in production mode is possible in some scenarios. Some impellers may have been previously coated to reduce product adherence and therefore potential build up, the effect of washing at full speed might significantly damage or remove this coating, and consequentially result in more frequent cleaning requirement.

Before undertaking this online cleaning, the option to stop and take off should be evaluated and full considerations taken of the possible consequences if control is lost. Impact forces alone might be sufficient to damage the fan blading and result in a total loss of both impeller and casing.

### 4.4 Bearing Selection for Fans

All fan bearings shall be designed to withstand the imposed design loads with a minimum L10 service life of 50,000 hours.

Typical large British Sugar fans are designed to be mounted on two pedestal mounted split Cooper roller bearings which will take high radial loads. One fixed (GR) on the side with the highest radial load and one Floating (EX) on the opposite side to allow for expansion during use (providing the fan and structure design allows for expansion in this direction).

Incorrect installation of the GR and EX bearings will potentially lead to premature failure due to extra resistance in the floating bearing caused by higher axial loads.

When fans are to be mounted on Cooper split bearings it is strongly recommended that an S1 fit is specified. This ensures the pedestal and cartridge are supplied as a matched pair which gives reduced clearances and in turn helps to reduce inherent vibrations. (this also means that when changing an existing pedestal or cartridge they must both be changed for another (S1) matched pair to maintain these reduced clearances)

Load, Speed, Temperature and Lubrication are all critical factors when selecting bearings.

Other considerations may apply such as component dimensions / space, cost and availability.

Bearing size is selected based on bearing load, speed and lubrication conditions.

Operating temperature is a result of the bearing load, speed, size, lubricating conditions and external influences.

Lubrication conditions depend on operating temperature, the viscosity of the lubricant and the speed.

It is important to specify a bearing with the correct load rating as a bearing with too little load will allow the rollers to skid against the races reducing its lifespan. A bearing which is overloaded will obviously have a reduced lifespan.

Temperature can be created by the material being transported (hot flue gas). Normal operating temperature for standard Cooper bearings is 0 to 100 deg. C. Increased internal clearance bearings designated with suffix C3 should be used higher temperature bearing applications. However the OEM of the bearings should be consulted to ensure the correct bearing is used for the application.

For operating temperatures up to 80 deg. C it is recommended standard mineral oil-based grease is used. For operating temperatures between 80 and 100 deg. C it is recommended a synthetic oil-based grease is used. For operating systems above 100 deg. C a circulating oil system with cooled and filtered oil is required. However, the OEM of the bearings and a greasing manufacturer should be consulted to ensure the correct lubrication for the application.

## 4.5 NDT of Fans

NDT of Fans is covered in STD-ENG-015 – However, during purchasing of a new fan it is sensible to ensure that the correct level of inspection is possible on the fan. This can be done by ensuring:

- There is a suitable and sufficient access hatch into the fan
- The welds on the fans can be masked off (general use of using masking tape to cover welds – suppliers may have different ways to achieve this) before any coating/painting is carried out, this will allow a thorough inspection of the welds when required
- Baseline NDT is carried out on new fans, this will highlight any defects, which may be picked up at a later inspection

## 4.6 Fan PPM Descriptions

It was agreed within the best practice and MPM group that Fan inspection PPMs should be built in the following way, this will enable standardisation across sites and aid in future data gathering exercises.

The PPM Description should be as follows:

**Fan Standard Inspection - Band 1 [Specifics] (STD-ENG-015)**

The band in the above description would change to suit. There may also be some specifics, e.g. where the site team has carried out an RBI and thus extended a period of inspection for a select group of fans or you have fans that would need scaffolds, examples of these could be:
