1.0 Introduction

This standard defines mandatory inspection and maintenance requirements for fans to ensure safety risks are tolerable.

2.0 Mandatory Policies

2.1 Inspection Requirements

Inspection requirements shall not be less than those recommended by the OEM.

| Band 2A            | Annual                   | As reqd.            |                   | Annual             |                   |                                               |
| Band 2B            | Annual                   | As reqd.            | Annual**          | Monthly            | Assess            | Every 2 Years ***                             |
| Band 3             | Annual                   | As reqd.            | Annual**          | Monthly            | Required          | Annual***                                     |

* *Not required if safety impact score = 1*  
** *Not required for non-metallic fans (also historically proven difficult on hollow metallic fans)*  
*** *RBI can be used to extend this frequency*

**Additionally,**  
Every fan shall be inspected Annually to ensure it is PUWER compliant, especially regarding fixed guarding.

All 2A/2B/3 band fans shall be routinely inspected in operational periods via Audio-Visual Inspection (AVIs). It is recommended that each fan is inspected weekly as a minimum.

2.2 Cooling Tower Fan Inspection Requirements

Cooling tower fans tend to have slower rotational speed, and despite larger diameters are unlikely to go above the 70m/s tip speed where we would normally consider fans to be in a higher band.

The hazard with these fans is from the fan breaking away, there is history of this occurring and components can take flight from the top of cooling towers. Thus the reason for having specific mandatory inspection tasks for these fans.

### 3.0 Fan Classification

Each fan shall be categorised using STD-ENG-015 Classification Table, defined in Appendix 1.

Fan banding is a measure of fan risk, determined by Likelihood of Failure and Consequence of Failure.

**Table 3:**

| Fan Band | Risk         | Comments                                                                                   |
|----------|--------------|-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| 0        | Minimal      | Impellor diameter < 300mm; Power input < 3.5kW. (Note: No LEV can be Band 0)              |
| 1        | Low          |                                                                                           |
| 2A       | Medium       |                                                                                           |
| 2B       | Medium/High  |                                                                                           |
| 3        | High         |                                                                                           |

---

**Likelihood of Failure (LoF)** is defined by the impellor tip speed* as follows:

- **Low LoF:** Tip Speed < 70 m/s
- **High LoF:** Tip Speed > 70 m/s

*Tip speed is calculated as:  
fan circumference (m) × (RPM/60) m/s

> Note: Where tip speed is < 70m/s but the LoF is considered high due to other factors (for example: arduous operating environment or history of failure), the LoF can be changed by the site fan competent/authorised person following the RBI process and recorded on the spreadsheet with comments.

---

**Consequence of Failure (CoF)** is defined by impellor diameter, as follows:

- **Low CoF:** Diameter < 1m
- **High CoF:** Diameter > 1m

> Note: Where impellor diameter is < 1m but the CoF is considered high due to other factors (for example: fan is business critical), the CoF can be changed by the site fan competent/authorised person following the RBI process and recorded on the spreadsheet with comments.

---

### 4.0 Risk Based Inspection

Mandatory inspection requirements shall not be changed unless authorised by the site fan competent/authorised person.

Any change to inspection scope or intervals can only be authorised if the fan risk-based inspection process defined within the RBI section of the Fan Classification Table (Appendix 1) has been followed.

Guidance notes on the RBI process to be followed are provided in Appendix 2.

Scope / intervals may be increased or reduced, dependent on several factors including: the level of experience and knowledge we have for a fan, the failure modes applicable, frequency of failure and consideration of the fan’s location.

7.0 **Continuous Monitoring**

Required on all Band 3 fans:

- Shall be a tripping type either vibration switch or electronic tripping system.
- These should be used solely for stopping the fan with serious out of balance.
- The vibration analysis should be used to trend failure over longer periods of time.
- Where these protection systems are used, if the purpose of the protection system is to provide protection to people, then integrity must be assessed using BS EN 61508 methods.
- Should the pre-set trip level be exceeded, the fan should be shut down, and only restarted once the cause has been ascertained and removed.
- If fans do not have tripping type monitoring a concession must be raised against the standard with full justification.
- Temperature monitoring should be installed when installing online vibration monitoring on Band 3 fans.

Band 2B fans should be considered, and risk assessed for continuous monitoring if the following apply:

- The fan is in close proximity to populated/occupied areas, e.g. at the doorway to a control room.
- The fan is in close proximity to high-risk items such as gas/fuel lines.
- Perceivable failure modes could introduce a serious risk to multiple persons, e.g. due to the size of the fan it is permissible an impeller rupture could send shrapnel over a large area.

These factors to fit continuous online monitoring does not override any initial risk assessments prior to installation of new fans, which may as part of their installations dictate the need for specific monitoring system set ups, for example the MVR fans at Wissington.

# HOW TO USE THE FAN CLASSIFICATION SPREADSHEET

This should be filled out with all fans (except those classed as Band 0 in the standard) to give the relevant banding.

Note that for each line, you must fill out all the white columns to get the result.

Fill in the fields as described below:

1. **CMMS No.**  
   Fill in this text field with a suitable code you may use to identify the fan, e.g. CMMS tag if applicable.

2. **Fan name**  
   Fill in this text field with the CMMS description of the fan e.g. No1 Drier ID Fan

3. **Location**  
   Fill in text here, describing the location of the fan being classified, e.g. first floor platform, AF drier building

4. **Diameter**  
   Input the diameter of the fan rotor, in metres

5. **Speed**  
   Input the speed, in rpm, of the fan rotor. For variable speed fans, input the maximum operating speed.

   *(Tip speed will now be calculated in the ‘greyed’ column F.)*

The following input columns are populated from a drop down list. Do this by first clicking on the cell causing a grey box with a down arrow on the right to appear on the cell. Then left click on this and select the option that applies to the column.

6. **Safety Impact**  
   Select whether the fan is located:
   1. In a Wall, Ceiling or Non-Pedestrianised area
   2. On a regular Pedestrianised route
   3. Next to a constant working area e.g. workshop

- **Risk** – Calculated automatically from the previous columns, as high or low.
- **Consequence** – Calculated automatically from the previous columns, as high or low.
- **Safety Impact** – Calculated automatically from the previous columns, as high, medium or low.
- **Band** – The band of fan, according to the fan standard ENG-STD-015, is shown in this column.

---

# HOW TO USE THE RBI CLASSIFICATION SPREADSHEET

For each Band 3 fan, (and lower bands if desired) fill out the spreadsheet considering each of the failure modes.

You will see that each fan has its failure modes listed; you will need to fill out each column for each failure mode to get the results calculated. This will be displayed in the ‘Action Needed’ column, which will indicate the importance of the mitigating controls listed to the right of the result. This can be used to decide on the course of action regarding those controls, e.g. high priority – carry out as per standard, low priority might mean the relevant inspection measure is only carried out every three years instead of annually.

Appendix 3: Health and Safety

Guarding:

Existing and New fans must provide adequate protection in terms of guarding from rotating and moving components. They must comply with the Provision and Use of Work Equipment Regulations 1998 (PUWER)

Noise:

Existing and New fans must comply with the Control of Noise at Work Regulations 2005. Fans installed in areas where hearing protection is mandatory should still demonstrate engineering best practice with regards to noise emissions.

Thermal Insulation:

Thermal insulation of fans is necessary when exposed surfaces can reach a temperature of greater than 50ºC in normal operation, or if required for economic reasons. If this applies, follow the guidance given in British Sugar standard on Thermal Insulation of Pipe and Vessels STD-ENGPROC-OTH-012.

---

Appendix 4: Procurement of New Fans

**Specification considerations depending on Fan Type**

| Band      | Requirements                                                                                                   |
|-----------|---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Band 1    | - Fan to be fit for purpose<br>- Corrosion considerations<br>- Flow performance<br>- Pressure Difference<br>- Input power requirement<br>- Fixed/variable speed operation<br>- Maintenance Frequencies and schedule<br>- Wear limits<br>- Maximum speed<br>- Balancing specification required (e.g. ISO.1940 or BS5265, G6.3 minimum requirement) |
| Band 2A/B | - As per Band 1 plus:<br>  - Pre-use inspection requirements<br>  - Overspeed test prior to despatch<br>  - Consider auditing manufacturer |
| Band 3    | - As per Bands 1 & 2 plus:<br>  - Online vibration monitoring/safety trip system risk assessment<br>  - Compulsory audit of manufacturer<br>  - Comprehensive maintenance and inspection manuals<br>  - Early life/commissioning tests |

---

Over speeding the fan to +10% of design speed before delivery helps to relax internal stresses and should be carried out by the supplier. This step may only be omitted if there is strong justification from the supplier to do so. Please note that over speeding the fans should NOT be done to any installed fan or any fan on site due to the need for precautions during testing.
