# 1.0 DESCRIPTION OF PLANT

**Air Supported Conveyor Belts** are belt conveyors where a substantial percentage of the load carrying strand of the belt is supported on an air cushion. Additional support is frequently provided by rollers situated at transfer, feed and discharge points.

**Troughed Belt Conveyors** are belt conveyors where the troughing of the belt is achieved by running it over troughed idler rollers.

**Non-Spill Belts** are troughed belt conveyors in which the troughing of the belt is achieved by running it over continuous flat plates. These flat plates are formed in such a way as to cause the belt to deform into a trough section. Due to the friction generated between the belt and slider plates these conveyors present a higher risk of product contamination than conventional troughed belts.

**Portable conveyors** are used extensively for the unloading of sugar silos and may be of either troughed or non-spill design.

## 1.1 The Scope of the Standard

1.1.1 The belt conveyor itself, including the main frame, drums, tensioning gear, idlers, covers, air blowers, cleaning devices and sensing devices.

1.1.2 The drive assembly, including drive frame, drip trays, gearboxes, couplings, guarding and motors.

1.1.3 Feed chutes, discharge chutes and dust extraction.

1.1.4 The supporting steelwork for the conveyor and drive unit.

### 3.0 INSPECTIONS

**Frequency: Daily**

The Belt shall be inspected to assess the condition of the following items:

- Covers & guards are in position and secure
- Oil leaks
- Sugar spillage
- Abnormal noise/Temperature from equipment
  - If heat is detected from casing (above 40°C) or motor, gearbox & bearings (above 80°C) then plant must be stopped without delay
- No abnormal levels of dust
- Check contents of collection trays on Clean Flo type belts
- Air pressure on air supported belts

*If any faults are found that represent an immediate risk plant must be stopped without delay.*

---

### 4.0 ROUTINES

#### 4.1 Routine Internal Inspection – All belt types (including portable when in use)

**Frequency: Up to, but not greater than 2 weekly**

The conveying plant shall be inspected to assess the condition of the following items:

- Condition of belt including joint and edges
- Belt tracking Return rollers [free to rotate and not worn]
- Fan and filter units on air supported belts
- Belt cleaning devices [brush box clear/brush adjustment]
- An internal inspection at both head and tail drum, via an appropriately sited inspection door [where applicable], to ascertain the belt position and condition
- Belt tension [GTU free to move or for screwed rod type, no excessive belt sag on the return strand]
- Air pressure on air knifes

6.0 MAINTENANCE

6.1 6 Monthly Maintenance

The conveying plant shall be inspected to assess the condition of the items listed, with the objective of identifying contamination risks. This shall include an internal inspection of the conveyor to identify product build-up which represents a contamination risk.

6.1.1 Covers and Guarding

1) Sufficient covers, guarding and connections should be removed to facilitate a thorough inspection and clean. Items removed should be clean and free of damage. All items when refitted must be secured and sealed.

2) Where there isn’t equipment downstream designed to catch lumps or contaminants then internal surfaces of feed chutes, discharge chutes and isolating slides should be checked and cleaned as necessary. Inlet and discharge chutes should be inspected internally and cleaned accordingly. Any damaged sealing strip should be replaced with an approved replacement (see 2.2.2).

6.1.2 Cleaning Devices

6.1.2.1 Belt Cleaning - Brushes

1) Check that the brush bristles are secure, clean and free from clogging.

2) Check correct operation of the brush mechanism and that it is correctly sequenced with the main conveyor.

6.1.2.2 Belt Cleaning - Scrapers

1) Check the scraper for signs of wear. The scraper should be correctly adjusted according to the manufacturer’s recommendations.

- Belt edge wear is excessive.

### 6.1.8 Skirts

1) Where side skirts are fitted [either at feed inlet points or along the length of the conveyor] then the skirts should be checked for signs of wear and deterioration and replaced accordingly.

2) All skirts should be at least 3 mm clear of the conveyor belt surface to ensure that there is no contact between the belt surface and the skirt.

3) All new skirting shall be a minimum of 6 mm in thickness and manufactured from white FDA food quality nitrile rubber.

4) Where belts are subject to localised increased loading, i.e. feed points, then the skirt should be supported with a Stainless-Steel backing plate, approximately 12 mm clear of the belt surface.

### 6.1.9 Product Build Up

1) Product build-up should be removed if its presence constitutes a contamination risk or could cause a malfunction of any part of the conveyor.

2) Brushes, scrapers, covers, drums, rollers, filters, inlet and outlet chutes should be examined.

3) Dust extraction points, and in particular ductwork which rise vertically from the conveyor, should be inspected and cleaned to avoid any build up from falling back into the product stream.

4) Ensure there are no abnormal levels of dust around the Belt.

5) All dust extraction pipework and connection joints must have dry gaskets and under no circumstances should mastic be used.

---

1) Drive: Motor & fan clean

2) Check Gear box oil level and clean drip trays

3) Undertake live oil samples in enough time before maintenance outages to determine oil change requirements

## 7.0 ADDITIONAL REQUIREMENTS FOR NON-SPILL CONVEYORS

The correct maintenance and operation of non-spill conveyors is critical because they present a greater risk of product contamination than standard troughed belt conveyors.

Particles of rubber or fibre from the belt are much more likely to be carried forward with the product because of the enclosed structure of a non-spill conveyor. There is also the additional risk of contamination with finely divided rubber particles arising from abrasion between the under surface of the belt and the slider plates, particularly at the joints in the plates.

---

### 7.1 **Slider Plates**

**These shall be manufactured from either Virgin PTFE or Stainless Steel.**

- **7.1.1** Virgin PTFE slider plates: these are normally bolted to a Stainless-Steel sub tray with tee bolts. It is important that any differential in height is smoothed out as far as possible.

- **7.1.2** If this is not possible the ends should be mitred to ensure that there are no steps against the flow of the belt.

- **7.1.3** The plate should be checked to ensure that wear has not reached the point of breaking through the surface and exposing the tee bolts.

- **7.1.4** If this has occurred then the plates shall be replaced.

- **7.1.5** Stainless Steel slider plates: these are less prone to distortion and wear.

- **7.1.6** The joints should be checked to ensure that there are no steps against the flow of the belt.

---

### 7.2 **Guide Rollers**

- **7.2.1** These feed the belt from the tail drum onto the slider plates.

- **7.2.2** Particular attention should be paid to checking that this roller is above the slider plate leading edge to prevent the belt from scraping the end of the slider plate.

---

### 7.3 **General**

General inspection of the conveyor shall take place with a view to identifying and modifying items that may result in damage to the belt surface, such as protruding bolt heads, sharp edges on framework, guards, etc.
